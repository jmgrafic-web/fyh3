Taxes When Buying
Property in Spain

A clear, honest breakdown of what you'll pay — so your budget is realistic before you fall in love with a property.

Updated January 2025 10 min read

The most common budget mistake international buyers make in Spain is underestimating purchase costs. The headline price is not what you pay. You also pay taxes, notary fees, land registry fees, and professional costs. In Catalonia, these typically add 12–15% to the purchase price for a resale property.

This guide covers everything — what each cost is, how it's calculated, and who you pay it to. We've written it specifically for buyers in Catalonia (Costa Brava, Maresme, Costa Dorada), where some rates differ from other Spanish regions.

Important disclaimer: This guide is for general information. Tax rates and rules change. Always confirm specifics with your Spanish tax advisor and gestor before signing. We are architects, not lawyers or tax advisors — but we've guided many clients through this process and know where the surprises tend to come from.

The Two Purchase Scenarios

The taxes you pay depend on whether you're buying a resale property (second-hand, from a private individual) or a new-build (from a developer, first transmission). The cost structures are different.

Resale Property: Taxes and Costs

Impuesto de Transmisiones Patrimoniales (ITP) — Property Transfer Tax

This is the main tax on resale properties in Spain. In Catalonia, the rate is 10% of the purchase price. This is paid to the Generalitat de Catalunya (regional government) within 30 working days of signing the escritura (deed).

Note: ITP is applied to the declared purchase price OR the tax authority's reference value (valor de referencia), whichever is higher. If the market value of your property is significantly above the reference value, you pay on what you actually paid. If the reference value is higher (unusual but possible), you may pay on that instead.

Impuesto sobre Actos Jurídicos Documentados (AJD) — Stamp Duty

When buying a resale property and paying ITP, you generally don't also pay the variable rate of AJD. There is a fixed element (document tax) which is minimal — typically under €200.

New-Build Property: Taxes and Costs

For new-builds (first transmission from developer), ITP does not apply. Instead you pay IVA (VAT) plus the variable rate of AJD.

CostRate / AmountNotesTypical %
IVA (VAT) 10% of purchase price Standard rate for residential new-builds in Spain. 10%
AJD (Stamp Duty) 1.5% in Catalonia Applied to the purchase price. Paid to regional government. 1.5%
Notary, Registry, Gestor As above Same as resale. 0.3–0.5%

Mortgage Costs (if applicable)

If you're financing your purchase with a Spanish mortgage, there are additional costs:

  • Bank appraisal (tasación): €300–€600, paid upfront to the bank's appointed valuer
  • Mortgage arrangement fee: 0–1% of the loan amount (varies by bank)
  • AJD on the mortgage deed: In Catalonia, the bank now pays this (changed in 2018)
  • Life insurance / home insurance: Often required by the bank

NIE number: You cannot complete a property purchase in Spain without a NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero). This must be obtained before signing. Allow 4–8 weeks if applying from abroad. Your lawyer can apply on your behalf with a power of attorney.

Ongoing Annual Costs

These are not purchase taxes but are important to factor into your total cost of ownership:

  • IBI (Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles): Annual property tax paid to the municipality. Typically €300–€2,500/year depending on property and location.
  • Basuras: Rubbish collection fee, typically €100–€300/year.
  • Community fees: If in a development, typically €100–€500/month.
  • Non-resident income tax (IRNR): If you're non-resident in Spain and don't rent the property, you still pay an imputed income tax — typically 1.1% of the cadastral value × 19% (EU/EEA residents) or 24% (non-EU). Usually a few hundred euros per year.
  • Wealth tax (Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio): In Catalonia, applies to net Spanish assets above €500,000. Rates from 0.21% to 2.75%. Structure advice from a tax advisor is important for higher-value purchases.

Common Budget Mistakes

  • Assuming 10% covers everything. ITP alone is 10%. Add notary, registry, lawyers, and the total is 12–15%.
  • Ignoring ongoing costs. IBI, non-resident income tax, insurance, community fees, and maintenance need to be factored into your annual holding costs.
  • Not accounting for currency risk. If you're buying in euros from a non-eurozone country, exchange rate movement between offer and completion can significantly change the real cost. A currency specialist (not your bank) typically offers significantly better rates.
  • Skipping the architect assessment. A €2,500 assessment that prevents a €60,000 structural surprise is not an optional extra — it's essential.
