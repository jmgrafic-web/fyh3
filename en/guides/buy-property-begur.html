How to Buy Property
in Begur

The market, the micro-locations, the risks, and the off-market reality — from architects who work here every day.

Updated January 2025 14 min read

Begur consistently ranks among the most desirable addresses on the Catalan Coast — and for demonstrable reasons. The combination of a preserved medieval village, six outstanding coves, exceptional natural scenery, and a firmly controlled building environment creates a market where demand consistently exceeds supply.

But buying here requires understanding how the market actually works, which micro-locations command premiums and why, what the risks are with older properties, and how to access properties that never reach the open market. This guide is our attempt to share what we've learned after years of working in Begur.

The Begur Market in 2025

Begur's market is characterised by structural undersupply. The municipality is surrounded by natural park land; building permits for new construction are extremely difficult to obtain; and the existing property stock turns over slowly because owners are reluctant to sell.

Average transaction volumes in Begur municipality are modest — typically 60–100 registered transactions per year across all property types and values. For context, a comparable-sized market in the Netherlands might see 5–10x that volume. This scarcity creates both opportunity (for buyers who move decisively) and risk (for buyers who don't do their due diligence).

Understanding the Coves — and Why They Matter for Buying

Where your property sits in relation to Begur's six coves is probably the most important factor in its value and character:

Sa Riera area

The most accessible cove, with the largest sandy beach. Properties in this zone benefit from the best family facilities and the most active summer scene. Rental potential is very strong. The trade-off is that it's also the busiest — less quiet in July and August.

Aiguablava / Fornells area

The premium zone. Aiguablava is widely considered one of the most beautiful bays in the Mediterranean. Properties here are extraordinarily rare — most have been in the same families for decades. When they do come to market, they often sell before listing publicly. Fornells offers a slightly more accessible entry point with its small marina.

Sa Tuna area

An authentic fishing cove with considerable charm. Less developed than the other coves, which is precisely its appeal. A growing number of discerning buyers are focusing here specifically because of the authentic character that remains.

Village centre

The medieval village itself — with its castle, narrow streets, and traditional architecture — offers very different properties. More affordable entry points but no direct cove access. Excellent rental potential from visitors who want the village experience. Best suited to buyers prioritising authenticity over beach proximity.

The Off-Market Reality

This is probably the most important thing to understand about the Begur market: the best properties are rarely listed.

Owners in Begur are typically wealthy, private, and in no rush to sell. They prefer to sell to someone they know — or someone introduced by someone they trust — rather than exposing their property to the market. Estate agents have limited access to this layer of the market. We have significantly better access because of our architectural work in the area — we often know when a property is coming available before the owner has spoken to an agent.

For buyers targeting Begur seriously, the most productive approach is to establish a clear brief with someone embedded in the local community and to wait. The right property rarely appears on a portal. It appears through the right conversation, at the right moment.

The Renovation Question

Many of the most desirable properties in Begur are older — stone village houses, rural properties converted from agricultural use, mid-century villas that have had piecemeal works over the decades. The opportunity in these properties is real: they offer character, location, and original features that cannot be created. The risk is equally real.

What renovation in Begur actually involves

  • All renovation works in Begur require a municipal licence — even minor interventions on historic buildings
  • Properties within the urban heritage protection zone are subject to additional restrictions on exterior changes, roof alterations, and window replacements
  • Rural properties (masies, casas de campo) are governed by agricultural land regulations — what can be done depends critically on the land classification
  • Access for construction machinery can be extremely difficult in the old town — this adds significant cost
  • Local craftsmen with heritage stone and lime render skills are in short supply — allow additional time

None of this should deter a buyer with the right intentions. It simply means that buying to renovate in Begur requires more planning, more time, and better advice than renovating a straightforward modern property elsewhere. We recommend commissioning an architectural assessment before any offer, followed by a feasibility study if the purchase proceeds.

Tourist Licences in Begur

The Ajuntament de Begur has imposed significant restrictions on new tourist rental licences. The current position (as of early 2025):

  • New tourist rental licences (llicències turístiques) are subject to a moratorium in most of Begur municipality
  • Existing licences attached to properties are transferable on sale — this is valuable
  • Properties in certain rural classifications may still be eligible for agrotourism licences under different rules
  • Long-term rental licences are not subject to the moratorium

If generating rental income from your property is part of your plan, verify the licence status as part of your due diligence — before any offer, not after.

How to Buy in Begur: Our Recommended Process

  1. Define your precise brief — cove preference, property type, budget (purchase + taxes + renovation), intended use (summer only vs extended stays), rental intent
  2. Establish relationships — with a buyer's agent or architect embedded in the market, with a Spanish lawyer independent of the seller, with a gestor for ongoing tax
  3. Search on and off market — portals will show you what's publicly available; relationships will show you what isn't
  4. Commission an architectural assessment before any firm offer — non-negotiable on older properties
  5. Verify the licence status if rental income matters to you
  6. Sign contrato de arras with 10% deposit, subject to legal checks and conditions agreed with your lawyer
  7. Complete at notary — typically 6–12 weeks after the arras
