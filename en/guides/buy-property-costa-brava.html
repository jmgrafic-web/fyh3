The Costa Brava is one of Europe's most extraordinary coastlines — and one of its most misunderstood property markets. This guide gives international buyers everything they need to understand the region, the process and the real opportunities available here.

Why buy property on the Costa Brava?

The Costa Brava runs 214km along the northeastern corner of Catalonia, from Blanes in the south to the French border at Portbou. It is defined by dramatic limestone cliffs, hidden coves of crystalline water, and a hinterland of cork forests and medieval towns that has been largely preserved from the mass-tourism development that has affected much of the Spanish coast.

For international buyers, the Costa Brava offers a combination that is genuinely rare in Europe: outstanding natural beauty, authentic local culture, exceptional food and wine, year-round accessibility, and property values that — despite significant appreciation over the past decade — remain competitive by comparison with equivalent locations in France, Italy or Portugal.

The property market in 2025

The Costa Brava market has matured significantly since 2015. Prices in prime towns (Begur, Tamariu, Llafranc, Cadaqués, Pals) have risen 40–65% over the decade, driven by strong international demand and very limited supply of quality properties. This is not a market with large new-build developments. Supply is constrained by geography, planning law and cultural resistance to over-development.

Current price ranges by property type:

  • Small apartments (renovation needed) in secondary locations: €180,000–€320,000
  • Character houses in prime towns (requiring work): €500,000–€900,000
  • Move-in ready character properties in prime locations: €800,000–€1.8M
  • Exceptional sea-view properties, fully renovated: €1.5M–€6M+

The greatest value opportunity lies in properties requiring architectural transformation. Our team specialises in identifying these — the structural bones are there, the views are extraordinary, the planning permission is achievable — but the finish needs updating. Buyers who invest €150,000–€300,000 in a well-conceived renovation can create properties worth significantly more than the purchase price plus costs.

The buying process in Spain

Buying property in Spain as a foreigner is entirely legal and relatively straightforward — but it differs from processes in Northern Europe in several important ways:

  • You will need a NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) — a Spanish tax identification number for foreigners
  • A Spanish bank account is required for the transaction
  • A Notario (public notary) handles the legal execution of the purchase deed
  • Transfer tax (ITP) in Catalonia is currently 10% of the purchase price for resale properties
  • Additional costs (notary, registry, legal fees) typically add 1–2% to total acquisition cost
  • Total "on-costs" run approximately 12–14% above the agreed purchase price

We strongly recommend engaging an independent legal representative (gestor or abogado) to conduct due diligence — checking title, outstanding debts, planning history and building licences. FYH coordinates all of this as part of our Buyer's Agent service.

Which town should I buy in?

This is the most important question for most buyers, and the answer depends entirely on what you want your life to look and feel like. The Costa Brava is not a single destination — it's a series of very distinct villages and towns, each with its own character, community and property market.

  • Begur — The architect's choice. A beautiful medieval hilltop town surrounded by the best calas on the coast. International community, exceptional character properties, strong year-round feel.
  • Tamariu — Arguably the most beautiful small bay on the Costa Brava. Exclusive, quiet, very limited property supply. Entry at €600,000+.
  • Llafranc — A proper beach village with a real community. Less exclusive than Tamariu but more convenient. Strong summer market.
  • Cadaqués — Completely unique. Whitewashed fishing village at the end of a dramatic mountain road. Strong arts heritage, Dalí connection. Limited but very loyal buyer community.
  • Pals — Medieval village with flat, rice-paddy hinterland and golf. Popular with Dutch and Belgian buyers. Excellent value relative to the coast.
  • Platja d'Aro / S'Agaró — More established resort infrastructure. Better for families who want amenities close by. Premium properties in S'Agaró specifically.

Frequently asked questions

Can EU and non-EU citizens buy property on the Costa Brava?

Yes. Spain imposes no nationality-based restrictions on property purchase. Both EU citizens and non-EU nationals (including UK, US, Australian, and Swiss buyers) can purchase freely. Non-EU buyers should be aware that property ownership alone does not automatically confer residency rights, though there are pathways (including the Golden Visa, where applicable) that can be explored.

Do I need to speak Spanish or Catalan?

Not with FYH. Our team operates fluently in English, Dutch, French and Catalan. We handle all communications with agents, lawyers, notaries, contractors and local authorities on your behalf. The property purchase process can be conducted entirely in your language.

How long does the purchase process take?

From agreeing a price to signing at the notary, the process typically takes 6–12 weeks. This includes the legal due diligence period, obtaining mortgage financing (if required), and the administrative steps around the NIE and bank account. In some cases, buyers have completed in 4 weeks; in others with complex title situations, it has taken 4–5 months.

We provide a Power of Attorney option that allows us to act on your behalf at the notary signing, and we document everything with photos, videos and regular written updates throughout the renovation.
