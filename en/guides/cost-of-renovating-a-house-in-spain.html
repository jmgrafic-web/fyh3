The Real Cost of Renovating
a House in Spain in 2024

Real numbers from architects on the ground. No optimistic estimates, no hidden surprises.

February 2024 14 min read Updated regularly

One of the most common mistakes international buyers make is underestimating renovation costs. They fall in love with a house — a ruined finca, a fisherman's house needing work, a 1970s villa — and then discover that what they thought was a €100,000 project is actually a €300,000 project. We see this happen regularly. This guide is our attempt to give you real numbers before you buy.

Important note: These figures apply specifically to Catalonia (Costa Brava, Maresme, Costa Dorada) in 2024. Costs vary significantly by location, access, material specification, and the state of the existing structure. Always commission an architectural assessment before committing to a purchase with renovation intent.

The four levels of renovation

Before looking at costs, it's useful to define what type of renovation you're dealing with. Spanish contractors and architects typically work with four levels:

Level 1: Cosmetic refresh (€200–400/m²)

New finishes only — flooring, painting, kitchen units, bathrooms. No structural work, no plumbing or electrical overhaul. Suitable for a well-maintained property that simply needs updating.

Level 2: Full interior renovation (€500–800/m²)

New electrical installation, updated plumbing, new kitchen and bathrooms, new flooring and finishes throughout. Structure intact. This is the most common type of renovation project FYH manages.

Level 3: Full renovation with structural work (€800–1,400/m²)

Includes Level 2 plus: opening walls, adding or reconfiguring rooms, structural reinforcement, facade work, new roof. Requires an architect-signed project and building permit.

Level 4: Rebuild or ruins restoration (€1,400–2,500+/m²)

Working from walls only, or a near-total reconstruction. Often the case with stone farmhouses (masies) or historic properties. Permits, heritage constraints, and structural engineers are always involved.

What drives costs up

Access

Rural properties with difficult access — narrow tracks, no crane access, distance from suppliers — add 15–25% to labour and materials costs. A house in Cadaqués or a remote inland finca will always cost more to renovate than the same house in a town centre.

Heritage and planning constraints

Properties in historic centres or with protected status face restrictions on materials, finishes and structural changes. These are not insurmountable, but they add time and cost. Heritage windows, specific lime mortars, traditional roof tiles — all of these have premium price tags.

Existing structure condition

Old Catalan stone construction can be superb — or hiding serious issues. Deteriorated wooden beams, rising damp, uneven foundations. These are the things an architectural assessment reveals. The difference between "the bones are good" and "the structure needs reinforcing" can be €50,000–150,000 in a medium-sized house.

Specification level

Material choices make an enormous difference. Italian porcelain tiles versus local ceramic. German kitchen appliances versus Spanish. Handmade joinery versus factory units. On a typical renovation, high specification adds 30–50% to the cost of mid-market finishes.

Additional costs to budget for

  • Architect fees: 8–12% of construction cost for full project + management
  • Building permits (licencia de obras): 2–4% of declared construction value
  • Technical inspections: €1,500–4,000
  • Project management: 5–8% of construction cost (included in FYH fees)
  • Contingency: Always budget 15–20% contingency on any renovation
  • Furniture and interior design: €500–1,500/m² depending on specification
  • Garden and pool: Pool installation €25,000–45,000; landscaping from €15,000

How long does it take?

Timelines in Catalonia have extended in recent years due to contractor demand. A realistic planning:

  • Permit application to approval: 3–9 months (depending on municipality and complexity)
  • Level 2 renovation (150m²): 4–6 months on site
  • Level 3 renovation (150m²): 6–10 months on site
  • Level 4 / ruins restoration: 12–24 months
